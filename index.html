Tread math

Treadmills are sold on peak horsepower - a number the motor can touch for a moment, not the duty it holds for your whole run. The deck gets the same treatment: "spacious" until a tall runner's stride hangs off the back. And the console's mph means nothing to a runner who thinks in minutes per mile. Tread math does the honest arithmetic: the sticker's peak becomes continuous duty, your height and plans become the deck you actually need, and the belt speed becomes the pace you know. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

The three tools

  • Motor decoder - the advertised peak HP becomes the honest continuous figure, banded from walking territory to a runner's motor.
  • Deck plan - your height and your plans (walk, jog, run) become the length and width that will not borrow your stride.
  • Pace translator - the belt's mph becomes minutes per mile and per km, exactly.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: the peak-to-continuous halving and the deck sizes are starting points - the spec sheet's own CHP figure, when the maker prints one, outranks any rule. Use the safety key every run, keep the deck clear behind the belt, and a used treadmill with a tired belt or a hot smell tells the truth no sticker can.
